# Microsoft Company Research Report

**Company:** Microsoft Corporation (Nasdaq: MSFT; CIK 0000789019). [SEC filing index](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/0001193125-26-323660-index.htm)  
**Prepared:** September 10, 2026.  
**Information cutoff:** July 29, 2026, after the FY2026 results and earnings call. Author-set convention matching the original report's earnings-date approach; this is not a current-price valuation.  
**Recommendation:** **Watch / defer initiation pending valuation.** UNSOURCED — author judgment.

## Investment view

**Author judgment:** Microsoft is a candidate for underwriting on the strength of its operating earnings, but a growth thesis needs to survive a cash-flow test. The key question is whether incremental cloud and AI spending earns an adequate return after equipment replacement, leases, taxes, and dilution. Strong demand alone does not establish an attractive purchase price.

## What the company does and growth drivers

Microsoft reports Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing. Q4 Azure growth was 43%; Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud grew 14%. [Earnings release, Business Highlights](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)

**Author inference:** Model enterprise software adoption and monetization separately from infrastructure consumption. Test whether incremental usage produces sustainable margins; avoid valuing all activities with one revenue multiple.

The cash-flow proxy is **not a reconciled FCFF input**: operating cash flow includes financing-related interest and other items, and cash capex does not capture all lease-financed investment. Weighted-average diluted shares are an EPS denominator, not automatically the valuation-date fully diluted count.

## Earnings quality and capital intensity

Q4 GAAP EPS was $4.81 versus $4.74 excluding OpenAI investment effects. That adjustment does not remove every unusual item: the release separately identifies an Anthropic investment gain. [Earnings release, reconciliation and discrete items](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)

Management reported Q4 capex of $41 billion, including $5.6 billion of finance leases, versus $35.8 billion cash PP&E spending. Commercial RPO was $678 billion; approximately 30% was expected within twelve months. [July 29 call, Amy Hood remarks](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)

**Author judgment:** Separate investment gains from recurring operations. Do not add RPO to enterprise value; it must convert into profitable revenue and cash. Reconcile lease-funded assets and obligations consistently before estimating FCFF or equity value.

## What must be resolved before underwriting value

The following are research questions and author judgments, not asserted company facts:

- What market price and fully diluted share count apply at a consistently chosen valuation timestamp?
- How much infrastructure spending maintains existing earnings versus creates new capacity?
- What happens to margins and cash conversion if AI utilization or pricing disappoints?
- How much liquidity is operationally necessary, and how should non-operating investments be valued without double counting their income?
- How should lease additions, principal, interest, and liabilities enter the model consistently?
- What discount rate follows from sourced market inputs, and how much value depends on terminal assumptions?
- Could competitive pricing, security incidents, or regulatory constraints undermine the forecast? These are proposed stress scenarios, not claims of specific events.

## Decision

**Watch / defer initiation — author judgment.** Proceed to a five-year FCFF DCF, reverse DCF, and comparable-company cross-check. An initiation decision requires a verified price comparison and a downside case that remains acceptable after reinvestment, lease, and dilution adjustments. No Microsoft target price has been calculated in this edition.
