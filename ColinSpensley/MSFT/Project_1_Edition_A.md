**User:** Investment Committee.  
**Company:** Microsoft Corporation (MSFT).  
**Valuation information cutoff:** July 29, 2026, after the earnings call. This is an author-selected historical information cutoff, not an assumed stock price.  
**Prepared:** September 10, 2026.  
**Initial decision:** **Watch / defer initiation pending valuation** — author judgment.

## Initial thesis

**Author judgment:** Underwrite Microsoft's operating franchise through the returns on incremental infrastructure and software investment. The bullish case requires growth to become distributable cash after reinvestment. The bearish case is that strong demand consumes capital without creating sufficient incremental value. A purchase decision must compare those outcomes against a verified market price.

## Consequential assumptions

All items below are author-selected modeling conventions, not sourced forecasts.

1. Use only information available by the stated cutoff. For a later valuation date, update market inputs and subsequent disclosures together.
2. Build a consolidated five-year FCFF model with segment operating drivers. Discount annual end-of-year cash flows and a Gordon-growth terminal value at the end of Year 5; require terminal growth below WACC.
3. Construct FCFF as EBIT × (1 − operating tax rate) + operating D&A − capex − change in operating working capital. Reconcile accounting classifications before entering the starting cash flow.
4. Treat operating cash flow less cash PP&E additions as a diagnostic, not an automatically valid FCFF estimate.
5. Add only non-operating assets excluded from operating forecasts. Deduct debt and lease obligations consistently with cash-flow treatment; do not subtract the same obligation twice.
6. Reconcile valuation-date dilution. Model stock compensation and repurchases without treating accounting add-backs as costless cash generation.
7. Source the WACC components and defend the growth fade and terminal rate independently. The class training assumptions are not Microsoft forecasts.

## Unknowns that could change the decision

- Valuation-date share price, capitalization, and a reconciled equity bridge.
- Durable segment growth and operating margins after incremental AI costs.
- Maintenance versus growth investment and useful-life assumptions.
- Cash conversion under lower infrastructure utilization or weaker monetization.
- Customer concentration and the timing and profitability of contractual commitments.
- Operating tax rate, normalized working capital, and non-operating investment value.

## Research plan

1. Select a market timestamp consistent with information availability; obtain price, risk-free yield, beta, equity risk premium, and debt cost.
2. Reconcile annual and quarterly segment data, including any changes in reporting definitions.
3. Build downside, base, and upside operating forecasts; show the revenue-to-FCFF bridge.
4. Reconcile cash, investments, borrowing, leases, and diluted shares to an equity bridge.
5. Run DCF and a WACC/terminal-growth sensitivity grid. Disclose terminal value's share of enterprise value.
6. Run a reverse DCF at the verified price and identify the implied operating expectations.
7. Cross-check with appropriate software and cloud peers, normalizing growth, margins, capital intensity, and accounting differences.
8. Recommend initiation only after comparing downside protection and potential return with the committee's requirements.
